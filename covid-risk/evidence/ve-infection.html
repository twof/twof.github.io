COVID event risk calculator

Evidence and methodology for model inputs

VE against infection

Default value: 25%

What this parameter means

VE (vaccine effectiveness) against infection is the reduction in probability that a vaccinated person becomes infected with SARS-CoV-2 compared to an unvaccinated person. A VE of 25% means a vaccinated person is 25% less likely to become infected upon exposure.

In this model, VE against infection is a property of the vaccine itself, not of any specific attendee group. It affects the source side of the transmission equation — specifically, how many attendees at the event are likely to be infectious:

  1. Prevalence reduction. Vaccinated attendees are less likely to be currently infectious. The effective prevalence is reduced by p × (1 − VE) + (1 − p), where p is the fraction of attendees with a current-season vaccine.
  2. Breakthrough infection composition. Among those who are infectious despite vaccination, the model determines what fraction are breakthrough infections (vaccinated) vs unvaccinated. Direct virological evidence for Omicron variants shows that breakthrough infections shed comparable amounts of virus to unvaccinated infections, so no additional shedding reduction is applied.

Higher VE against infection means fewer infectious people at the event, which reduces risk for everyone.

Evidence

2024–2025 vaccine effectiveness against infection

A study of 538,631 matched pairs in the US Veterans Health Administration measured the effectiveness of the 2024–2025 KP.2 COVID-19 vaccine against laboratory-confirmed SARS-CoV-2 infection over long-term follow-up:

6031.3%25.6–36.4%
9025.8%20.9–29.8%
12022.4%17.7–26.0%
232 (end of follow-up)16.6%11.9–21.4%

VE waned at approximately 0.15 percentage points per day, declining from ~31% at 2 months to ~17% at 8 months. The study population was predominantly older male veterans (mean age 70.7 years, 90.9% male).

VE is similar across age groups and diverse populations

The CDC MMWR (2025) reported VE against COVID-19–associated ED/UC visits from the VISION and IVY networks (137,543 encounters across 20 US states, September 2024–January 2025). Results stratified by age:

  • 18–64 years: 30% (95% CI 20–39%)
  • ≥65 years: 35% (95% CI 29–41%)

This general healthcare-seeking population (median age 53, 60% female) showed VE similar to the veteran cohort, suggesting age does not dramatically change VE against infection for the current vaccine.

A 2025 study published in JAMA Internal Medicine measured VE against infection specifically (not just medically attended outcomes) and found a steeper waning curve: 44.7% at 4 weeks, 35.5% at 10 weeks, and 16.7% at 20 weeks — converging to the same ~15–20% floor regardless of population.

VE against infection is much lower than VE against severe outcomes

For the same 2024–2025 vaccine season, effectiveness against more severe outcomes was substantially higher (NEJM 2025, MMWR 2025):

  • ED visits: 29%
  • Hospitalization: 40%
  • ICU admission: 60%
  • Mechanical ventilation or death: 79%

This gradient — modest protection against infection but strong protection against severe disease — is consistent across the pandemic since Omicron. The vaccine’s primary benefit is now reducing severity rather than preventing infection.

Historical context: VE was much higher earlier in the pandemic

A systematic review and meta-analysis found VE against infection decreased from 68.9% for the primary series to 38.9% after 6 months, with boosters restoring it to 76.4% (declining to 58.4% after 3 months). These higher values applied to earlier variants and vaccine formulations. Current VE against infection is substantially lower due to Omicron lineage immune evasion and population-level hybrid immunity.

Is the default of 25% reasonable?

Yes. The 2024–2025 data shows VE against infection of 31% at 2 months declining to 22% at 4 months. A default of 25% corresponds to approximately 3 months post-vaccination — a reasonable midpoint for an event-attending population with varied vaccination timing.

Users should consider adjusting this value based on:

  • Time since the latest vaccine update: Shortly after a new formulation is released, VE may be higher (~30%); several months later, it may be lower (~15–20%).
  • Circulating variant: If a significantly immune-evasive variant emerges, VE against infection may drop further.

Limitations

  • Single value for all vaccinated attendees. In reality, VE varies by time since vaccination, number of prior infections, age, and immune status. The model uses one value as a population average.
  • Source-side only. This parameter affects the number of infectious attendees at the event. The susceptible person’s own vaccine-derived protection against infection is captured separately through the hybrid immunity parameter.
  • Waning is not modeled dynamically. The model uses a snapshot VE value. In practice, VE at any given event depends on how recently attendees were vaccinated. With annual vaccine updates, there is a seasonal pattern of higher VE shortly after rollout and lower VE months later.
