COVID event risk calculator

Evidence and methodology for model inputs

IC prevalence by age and severity mix

How it affects the model

These two parameters determine how many IC attendees are expected at the event and how sick they are. The age-specific IC prevalence is combined with the event’s age distribution and self-selection factor to estimate the number of IC attendees. The severity mix then allocates those IC attendees across severe, moderate, and mild tiers, which have different hospitalization and long-COVID rates per infection.

IC prevalence by age

Source: Martinson et al. 2024 (JAMA)

Martinson et al. (2024) estimated the prevalence of immunosuppression among US adults using the 2021 National Health Interview Survey (NHIS), a nationally representative sample of 29,164 adults (2,123 with immunosuppression). Overall prevalence: 6.6% (95% CI: 6.2–6.9%).

The model values match Martinson almost exactly. Prevalence peaks at 60–69 (9.5%) and declines in the oldest age band — likely because some immunosuppressive conditions (cancer, autoimmune disease) are treated less aggressively in the very elderly.

Key context

  • The 6.6% prevalence is more than double the earlier widely cited 2.7% estimate from 2013 NHIS data (Harpaz et al. 2016). The increase reflects the growing use of immunosuppressive medications for autoimmune diseases, not a true increase in immunodeficiency.
  • The NHIS uses self-report: “Has a doctor ever told you that you have a weakened immune system?” plus questions about immunosuppressive medications. This is broad and may include people with mild immunocompromise (e.g., low-dose methotrexate for rheumatoid arthritis).
  • Prevalence is higher in women (7.9%) than men (5.2%), driven by higher autoimmune disease rates in women.

IC severity mix

Default: 5% severe, 20% moderate, 75% mild.

No published source directly gives a severe/moderate/mild breakdown of the IC population using these specific tiers. The model’s tiers are defined by COVID-specific hospitalization risk, mapped from INFORM study aIRRs:

TierINFORM aIRRConditions
Severe10–14×Organ transplant, stem cell transplant (≤2yr), active hematological malignancy, severe primary immunodeficiency
Moderate4–6×ESKD, autoimmune on strong immunosuppressants (biologics, high-dose DMARDs), primary immunodeficiency, unsuppressed HIV
Mild~1.4×Solid tumor survivors (≤5yr), chronic corticosteroids, mild immunosuppressive therapy, virally suppressed HIV

This gives roughly 775K severe (4%), 3.3M moderate (18%), 14.7M mild (78%) — a population dominated by mildly immunocompromised individuals. The model rounds to 5% / 20% / 75%.

Cross-check against INFORM

The INFORM study (Quint et al. 2023) found an overall aIRR of 2.04 (1.95–2.14) for IC vs non-IC hospitalization. The blended aIRR implied by the severity mix provides a consistency check:

The remaining gap (2.6× vs 2.04×) may reflect that the event population skews younger than the INFORM all-ages cohort, or that the condition-count estimates above slightly overweight moderate conditions. The match is reasonable given the uncertainties.

Limitations

  • Self-report definition. The Martinson prevalence (6.6%) is based on self-report, which may include people whose immunosuppression is clinically negligible. The INFORM study used clinical records and found 3.9% IC prevalence in England. The US self-report rate is higher partly because more Americans take immunosuppressive medications for common conditions.
  • Severity tiers are model-specific. No published source classifies IC individuals into these exact three tiers. The mapping from conditions to tiers involves judgment calls, especially for moderate vs mild (e.g., where to place someone on moderate-dose methotrexate).
  • Condition counts are approximate. Several estimates (autoimmune on strong immunosuppressants, chronic corticosteroids) are rough order-of-magnitude figures, not precise counts from a single authoritative source.
  • Event self-selection may shift the mix. IC individuals who attend events may not be representative of the IC population. Very severely IC people may avoid events entirely (captured by the self-selection factor), while mildly IC people may not even identify as IC. The mix at any specific event depends on the community.
  • The mix changes with age. Older IC individuals are more likely to have cancer or ESKD (moderate to severe), while younger IC individuals are more likely to have autoimmune conditions on mild therapy. The model uses a single mix for all ages.
