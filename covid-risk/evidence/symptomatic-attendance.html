COVID event risk calculator

Evidence and methodology for model inputs

Symptomatic attendance probability

Default: 0% — assumes a strict no-symptoms policy where symptomatic individuals do not attend.

How it affects the model

Not all infectious people know they are infectious. Some have no symptoms at all; others haven’t developed symptoms yet. This input controls the one part event organizers can influence: whether people with active symptoms attend.

The model computes the fraction of currently-infectious people who would attend the event by combining three groups:

  1. Asymptomatic — fully infectious but will never develop symptoms. They always attend.
  2. Presymptomatic — infectious but symptoms haven’t appeared yet. They attend unknowingly.
  3. Symptomatic — have active symptoms. Whether they attend depends on event policy and individual behavior (this slider).

Because asymptomatic infections clear faster than symptomatic ones (3 vs 5 days), asymptomatic people make up a smaller share of the currently-infectious pool than their incidence fraction suggests. The model accounts for this by weighting each group by its infectious duration:

With a strict no-symptoms policy (slider at 0%), 56% of infectious people would still attend. With no policy and 50% of symptomatic people showing up, 78% would attend.

Evidence

Asymptomatic fraction: 50%

Choi et al. (2025) developed a compartmental transmission model calibrated against Korean national COVID-19 data from 2020–2022. They estimated the probability of asymptomatic infection by period and vaccination status:

PeriodUnvaccinatedVaccinated
Period I (ancestral, pre-vaccine)31.6%—
Period II (Delta)37.6%41.7%
Period III (Omicron)44.7%49.7%

Two clear trends: the asymptomatic fraction increased with each variant, and vaccination consistently raised it. For a 2026 population with near-universal hybrid immunity (vaccination + prior infections), 50% is likely conservative.

An earlier meta-analysis of 8 Omicron-era studies (Ma et al. 2022) found a pooled asymptomatic fraction of 32.4% (95% CI: 25.3–39.5%), but this included unvaccinated populations and predates widespread hybrid immunity. It also noted higher rates with vaccination coverage ≥80% (35.9%) and younger populations (43.8%).

Asymptomatic infectious duration: 3 days

Choi et al. (2025), Table 2: the recovery period for vaccinated asymptomatic Omicron cases was 3 days, compared to 5 days for unvaccinated asymptomatic cases. This shorter clearance time means that even though 50% of new infections are asymptomatic, at any snapshot only ~37.5% of currently-infectious people are asymptomatic.

Symptomatic infectious duration: 5 days

Singanayagam et al. (2022) prospectively followed 57 community COVID-19 cases (ATACCC study, UK) with daily viral culture sampling. The median duration of infectious virus shedding was 5 days (IQR 3–7). 65% of cases still shed infectious virus at day 5 post-symptom onset, dropping to 24% at day 7.

This study used pre-Omicron/Delta variants. Omicron infectious periods may be slightly shorter, making 5 days a conservative estimate for total symptomatic infectious duration.

Presymptomatic infectious window: 1.5 days

The presymptomatic infectious period is the gap between the onset of infectious shedding and the onset of symptoms. For Omicron:

  • The incubation period (infection to symptoms) is ~3–3.4 days (Tan et al. 2023, Singapore).
  • The serial interval (infector symptoms to infectee symptoms) is ~2–3 days, shorter than the incubation period. This implies substantial presymptomatic transmission (Guo et al. 2023: 62% of Omicron transmission was presymptomatic).
  • However, the ATACCC viral culture study found only 20% of cases shed actual infectious virus before symptom onset, even though 63% had PCR-detectable RNA presymptomatically. This suggests the presymptomatic infectious window is short.

The model uses 1.5 days as a compromise: infectiousness likely begins ~1–2 days before symptoms for Omicron, consistent with both the serial interval data and the viral culture evidence.

Limitations

  • Asymptomatic fraction is extrapolated forward. The 50% estimate is from early Omicron (Jan–Jun 2022) in vaccinated individuals. With 4 additional years of immune maturation and variant evolution, the true value in 2026 could be higher. No current surveillance system measures this directly.
  • Infectious durations are from earlier variants. The 5-day symptomatic duration is from pre-Omicron/Delta data (ATACCC). The 3-day asymptomatic duration is from a model calibrated to Korean Omicron data. Neither has been validated for 2026 variants, though the direction of bias (shorter with more immunity) would make our estimates conservative.
  • Presymptomatic window is uncertain. Viral culture studies suggest it may be as short as 0–1 days, while epidemiological modeling suggests 1–2 days. The model uses 1.5 days as a middle estimate.
  • Symptomatic attendance is behavioral. The slider default of 0% assumes a strict policy. In practice, people with mild symptoms (slight sore throat, mild congestion) may not recognize them as COVID or may attend anyway. The true symptomatic attendance rate depends on event culture, enforcement, and individual judgment.
  • “Asymptomatic” definition varies. Some studies define asymptomatic as no symptoms at any point; others include very mild or “paucisymptomatic” cases. The Choi et al. model uses the strict definition (never develops symptoms).
