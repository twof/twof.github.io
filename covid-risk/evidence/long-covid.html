Evidence and methodology for model inputs

P(long COVID | infection)

The model uses separate long-COVID tables for IC attendees (stratified by severity and vaccination status) and non-IC attendees (stratified by vaccination status only).

Definition of long COVID

The model uses the 2024 NASEM definition:

“An infection-associated chronic condition that occurs after SARS-CoV-2 infection and is present for at least 3 months as a continuous, relapsing and remitting, or progressive disease state that affects one or more organ systems.”

Key features of this definition:

  • Can follow asymptomatic, mild, or severe infection
  • Does not require laboratory confirmation of prior COVID-19
  • Can be delayed in onset for weeks or months after apparent recovery
  • Ranges from mild to severe; can resolve over months or persist for years
  • Intentionally inclusive (high sensitivity, lower specificity)

The WHO definition is similar: symptoms starting within 3 months of infection, lasting at least 2 months.

Important: published long-COVID prevalence estimates vary enormously (2–35%) depending on which definition is used, how symptoms are ascertained (self-report vs clinical diagnosis), and the comparison group. The model calibrates to the stricter end of the range (NASEM-type clinical condition, not “any persistent symptom”).

How it affects the model

These values convert expected infections into expected long-COVID cases. Like hospitalization rates, they are applied after the Wells-Riley infection probability and do not affect the infection estimate itself.

Calibrating to the NASEM definition

The community cohort’s 8% (vaccinated, 90 days) uses a broad symptom survey. The NASEM definition is stricter (clinically significant condition affecting organ systems, not just “any persistent symptom”). Recent 2024 WHO/CDC estimates place long COVID at 2–7% of infections in the current context of widespread hybrid immunity and Omicron-lineage variants.

The model uses values at the lower-to-middle end of this range, reflecting the stricter NASEM definition:

IC long-COVID rates

The IC multiplier is smaller than for hospitalization

Unlike hospitalization (where IC patients face 2–14× elevated risk), the long-COVID multiplier for IC is modest. Englesbe et al. (2024) studied PASC in solid organ transplant (SOT) recipients using the National COVID Cohort Collaborative (n = 17,512 matched pairs):

GroupPASC prevalenceaOR vs non-IC
SOT recipients2.2%1.48 (1.09–2.01)
Non-immunosuppressed1.4%(baseline)

This ~1.5× elevation is biologically plausible: long COVID is partly driven by immune overactivation and autoimmunity, mechanisms that IC patients may have less of, partially offsetting their higher infection severity and prolonged viral persistence.

Mapping to IC severity tiers

The 1.48× aOR is for transplant recipients (the model’s “severe” tier). The multiplier for milder IC conditions is likely smaller. The model applies graduated multipliers:

  • Severe IC (transplant, active hematological malignancy): ~2.0–2.5× non-IC rates. Higher than the SOT study’s 1.48× to account for prolonged viral shedding in the most immunosuppressed patients.
  • Moderate IC (immunosuppressive therapy, PID): ~1.5–2.0×
  • Mild IC (solid tumors, corticosteroids): ~1.25–1.5×

Age adjustment for non-IC long-COVID rates

Unlike hospitalization (which increases ~12× from youngest to oldest adults), long-COVID risk has a much flatter age gradient and peaks in middle age rather than the eldest. MEPS Spring 2023 data (among adults who ever had COVID-19):

The decline in the 65+ bracket is consistent with the hypothesis that long COVID is partly driven by immune overactivation, which older adults (and IC patients) may mount less aggressively. The model applies these multipliers to the non-IC base rates, which are calibrated for 18–39 year olds.

Limitations

  • Definition-dependent prevalence. Published long-COVID rates range from 2% to 35% depending on the definition, ascertainment method, and comparison group. The model calibrates to the NASEM clinical definition, but this involves a subjective adjustment from broad-symptom survey data.
  • Limited IC-specific data. The SOT PASC study (aOR 1.48) is the best available IC-specific estimate, but it covers all IC severity levels and uses a single transplant cohort. Long-COVID rates may differ substantially for other IC conditions (e.g., HIV, autoimmune disease).
  • Evolving over time. Long-COVID rates have declined with successive variants and increasing population immunity. The 2024 estimate of 2–7% may continue declining. The model’s values reflect 2024–2025 Omicron-lineage data.
  • No severity stratification within long COVID. The model counts any NASEM-qualifying long COVID equally. In reality, long COVID ranges from mild persistent fatigue to severe multi-organ dysfunction. A more refined model would weight by severity, but the data to do so is sparse.
  • Vaccination timing matters. VE against long COVID wanes similarly to VE against infection. The model uses broad “current” vs “old” categories rather than a continuous waning curve.
