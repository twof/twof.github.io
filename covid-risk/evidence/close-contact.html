COVID event risk calculator

Evidence and methodology for model inputs

Close-contact / near-field exposure

Default: 4 close-range neighbors per attendee at an average distance of 1.0 m (rows-of-chairs configuration).

What this models

The base Wells-Riley model assumes the room air is well mixed and that infectious aerosols dilute uniformly into the room volume. That assumption holds for the far-field contribution — the background concentration that builds up as ventilation flushes the room — but it understates the dose to anyone close enough to an infectious person to be inside their exhaled plume.

This input adds a parallel near-field exposure term. For each close-range neighbor (within ~1.5 m) that turns out to be infectious, the receiver is exposed to a local concentration higher than the room average, with the local enhancement set by the two-stage exhaled-jet dilution model.

Mathematical form

The total per-event Poisson exposure rate is the sum of two terms:

rate_total = rate_far + rate_near

  • rate_far = (attendees - 1) · p_inf · D_far where D_far is the existing well-mixed Wells-Riley per-source dose: q · t · b · transient(t, ACH) / (ACH · V).
  • rate_near = N_close · p_inf · D_near where D_near = q · t · b / (Q_mouth · S(L)) and S(L) is the jet dilution factor at distance L from the emitter's mouth.

Per-event probability: P(inf) = 1 − exp(−rate_total).

Receiver-side mask filtration and source-side mask emission reduction apply identically to both terms (the same physics filters particles regardless of geometry). The eating-time mask-removal split that the model already applies for the far-field term applies to the near-field term too.

Jet dilution table (Jia et al. 2022)

The dilution factor S(L) is the ratio of mouth-orifice concentration to receiver-location concentration. Standard / sedentary activity, puff-stage values, log-log interpolated:

Distance (m)Dilution S(L)Local conc. vs. room avg.
0.33extreme
0.512very high
1.094high
1.5350approaching room average

The transition from jet (1/L decay) to puff (1/L2) stage occurs at 0.28–0.99 m depending on activity intensity, per Jia’s Equations 7 and 13. Cortellessa et al. 2021 independently report that close-proximity risk merges with the well-mixed-room model at interpersonal distances of 1.4–1.6 m — consistent with the table above (S(1.5) = 350 starts to approach the typical room-average dilution).

Mouth-exhalation volume rate (calibration)

The model uses Q_mouth = 1.0 m³/hr to convert the time-averaged quanta-emission rate q into a mouth-orifice concentration. This value is the only free calibration parameter and was chosen so the model reproduces two independent published anchors:

  • Cortellessa 2021: 15-min face-to-face speaking at 1 m (median emitter) → ~1% per-encounter infection probability. Model reproduces 1.06%.
  • Hu 2024: typical university classroom (~28 students, ~150 m³, 0.9 m mean interpersonal distance) → 40.5% near-field share of total dose. Model reproduces ~47%.

Both anchors converge on the same Q_mouth value, which is itself plausible: the average exhaled-air volume rate during a breathing cycle for a sedentary adult is ~0.5 m³/hr; during normal speech it is somewhat higher because exhalation is sustained. Q_mouth = 1.0 sits within that physical range.

What the new term changes

At the calculator’s defaults (100 attendees, 10,000 m³, ACH 3, 4 close at 1 m, no masks), the near-field component dominates total dose because the large well-ventilated hall heavily suppresses the far-field contribution but leaves the near-field contribution unchanged.

More importantly, the near-field term decouples total risk from room volume. Doubling the room volume reduces the far-field component by half but leaves the near-field component unchanged — total risk drops only a few percent rather than ~50%. This addresses a long-standing limitation of the well-mixed Wells- Riley model that has been increasingly criticized in the literature since 2021.

Caveats and limitations

  • The jet dilution table is for standard / sedentary activity. More vigorous activity (loud speech, singing) shifts the jet–puff transition outward, which would increase near-field dose; the model implicitly handles this through the higher quanta rates for those activities, but does not adjust S(L) per activity.
  • The model assumes the same close neighbors are present throughout the event. Real seating may rotate (intermission, food service); the parameter should be interpreted as time-averaged.
  • Face-to-face vs face-to-back orientation matters in CFD models. Hu 2024 found 87% face-to-back in classrooms, which softens the close-contact dose vs face-to-face. The single dilution table here is an effective average; a per-orientation model would be more accurate but adds another parameter.
  • Outdoor or strongly directional ventilation (e.g. UV-cleaned air, displacement ventilation) can disrupt the puff-stage dilution and reduce the near-field contribution. Not modeled.

Sources

  • Henriques et al., J R Soc Interface 2025 — “An integrated airborne transmission risk assessment model for respiratory viruses: short- and long-range contributions.” Multi-box (short + long) continuum model, adopted by WHO ARIA. Reports short-range dose 10× long-range in patient wards.
  • Jia et al., Building and Environment 2022 — “Exposure and respiratory infection risk via the short-range airborne route.” Two-stage jet model and dilution-factor table used here.
  • Cortellessa et al., Sci Total Environ 2021 — “Close proximity risk assessment for SARS-CoV-2 infection.” Establishes the 1.4–1.6 m boundary distance and provides the 15-min/1-m calibration anchor.
  • Hu et al., Sci Total Environ 2024 — “Analysis of SARS-CoV-2 transmission in a university classroom based on real human close contact behaviors.” Real-data classroom decomposition (40.5% short-range / 59.5% long-range); near-field-share calibration anchor.
  • WHO Airborne Risk Indoor Assessment (ARIA) tool, 2024. Endorses the multi-box short+long approach for indoor risk assessment.
  • ASHRAE Standard 241-2023, Control of Infectious Aerosols. Explicitly covers only long-range (>1 m) airborne transmission, treating close contact as a separate problem — the regulatory motivation for the two-zone split.
  • CAiMIRA (CERN open-source implementation). Reference codebase that combines Wells-Riley with a two-stage jet term in production.
