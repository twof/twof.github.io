COVID event risk calculator

Evidence and methodology for model inputs

IC vaccination / PrEP mix

Default (regimented): 2% vax+PrEP, 30% current vax only, 68% old vax, 0% none. Assumes all attendees have at least the original vaccine series.

How it affects the model

The IC vaccination mix determines two things:

  1. Outcome severity — hospitalization and long-COVID probabilities per infection are lower for currently vaccinated IC individuals, and lowest for those also on PrEP (Pemgarda).
  2. Source-side transmission — the fraction with current vaccination contributes to the blended vaccine-mediated reduction in community transmission (via reduced viral shedding).

Evidence

IC vaccination uptake is low and mirrors the general population

The assumption that immunocompromised individuals vaccinate at high rates is not supported by the data. Despite clinical recommendations for multiple annual doses, IC vaccination uptake is only modestly above general population levels.

CDC NIS-FRVM: 2024–2025 season (most recent)

The CDC’s National Immunization Survey tracks COVID-19 vaccine uptake by self-reported high-risk health condition (which includes but is broader than immunocompromised). As of April 2025:

Uptake plateaued by December 2024 at ~33% for high-risk adults and ~17% for non-high-risk. High-risk individuals vaccinate at roughly 2× the general population rate, but two-thirds still do not get the current-season vaccine.

McGrath et al. 2025: IC-specific uptake in the US

McGrath et al. (2025) studied 365,146 immunocompromised individuals in California and Louisiana during the 2023–2024 season. Among IC individuals who had previously received at least one COVID vaccine:

  • California: 20.8% received the 2023–2024 XBB dose
  • Louisiana: 6.1%

By IC condition: HIV/AIDS 29.2%, hematologic malignancy 26%, transplant 19.3%, immunosuppressive therapy 19.3%, primary immunodeficiency 20.5%.

Tartof et al. 2023: IC uptake at Kaiser Permanente

Tartof et al. (2023) studied 42,697 IC patients at Kaiser Permanente Southern California — a best-case integrated health system. By August 2022, only 41% had received 4 doses and 1% had received 5 doses. The lead author called this “a widespread problem” even at Kaiser.

UK comparison: NHS proactive outreach achieves much higher uptake

The INFORM study (Quint et al. 2025) analyzed 459,230 IC individuals in England during 2023. 70.2% had received ≥4 COVID vaccine doses, compared to 29.4% of the general population (2.4× ratio). The NHS proactively identifies and invites IC patients through GP registries — a fundamentally different delivery model than the US, where IC patients must self-navigate to a pharmacy.

This suggests the low US IC vaccination rate reflects systemic access and delivery barriers, not inherent hesitancy. When the healthcare system actively reaches out, IC individuals do vaccinate at substantially higher rates.

PrEP (Pemgarda/pemivibart) uptake is negligible

Pemgarda (pemivibart) was authorized in March 2024 for pre-exposure prophylaxis in moderately to severely IC individuals. The only published real-world study (IDWeek 2025) included just 48 patients across the entire US over one year. PrEP requires specialist referral and periodic infusions, limiting uptake to a tiny fraction of the IC population.

Why is IC vaccination uptake so low?

Multiple barriers have been identified:

  • Vaccine fatigue — each successive formulation sees lower uptake. The decline is consistent across all populations, not IC-specific.
  • Perceived futility — 44% of hesitant IC patients doubt their immune system can mount a response, asking “why bother if it won’t work for me?” (Kelso et al. 2022)
  • Safety concerns — 60% of unvaccinated blood cancer patients said the vaccine was “too risky for their health”; 52% worried about side effects (LLS survey via ASH Clinical News).
  • Miscommunication — 51% of unvaccinated blood cancer patients reported their oncology provider had recommended against vaccination, though this may reflect miscommunication rather than actual medical advice.
  • Schedule complexity — the IC vaccination schedule (3+ doses per season, timed around treatment) is confusing. A Delphi panel found only 56–69% of physicians agreed on timing relative to treatments (PMC11499552).
  • Delivery model — in the US, the specialist managing the IC condition typically doesn’t administer vaccines. Vaccination requires a separate pharmacy appointment, with no proactive outreach system.

IC behavioral precautions are also modest

A KFF COVID-19 Vaccine Monitor survey of IC adults found:

  • 40% still wear masks (60% do not)
  • 11% avoid crowds
  • 17% have made no behavioral adjustments at all
  • 89% trust their oncologist for COVID information

Most IC individuals’ behavior is only modestly more cautious than the general population, despite genuinely elevated risk.

Non-IC vaccination defaults

The non-IC defaults (25% current-season, 75% older vaccination, 0% none) are based on:

  • CDC NIS-FRVM 2024–2025: overall adult uptake of the current-season vaccine was ~23%, plateauing by early 2025. The non-high-risk adult rate was 17%. We use 25% as a slight upward adjustment assuming event attendees skew slightly more health-engaged than the general population.
  • The “none” category is set to 0% based on the assumption that all event attendees have at least the original vaccine series.

Limitations

  • The NIS “high-risk” category is broader than IC. It includes asthma, diabetes, and heart disease. The IC-specific rate could differ. McGrath’s IC-specific 21% (CA, 2023–2024) is the best direct measurement.
  • Event selection effects. IC individuals who attend events with COVID precautions may be more health-conscious than the IC population average. The defaults may underestimate vaccination rates for this self-selected subset.
  • Geographic variation. California (21%) and Louisiana (6%) show enormous state-level differences. SF/Bay Area uptake was noted as higher than the CA average in McGrath.
  • The US-UK gap suggests these are system-dependent, not population-inherent. IC vaccination rates are a reflection of healthcare delivery, not patient attitudes. In systems with proactive outreach (UK NHS), IC individuals vaccinate at 70%.
