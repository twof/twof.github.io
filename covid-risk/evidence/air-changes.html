COVID event risk calculator

Evidence and methodology for model inputs

Air changes per hour (ACH)

Default value: 3.0

How it affects the model

ACH enters the model in two places:

  1. Steady-state concentration. C = q / (ACH × V). Higher ACH means more air replacement, lower airborne quanta concentration, and lower inhaled dose. At low infection probabilities, doubling ACH approximately halves the risk.
  2. Transient buildup rate. The transientDoseFactor depends on ACH × duration. Higher ACH means concentration reaches steady state faster. At ACH = 3 and a 2-hour event, the transient correction is ~0.83 (concentration spends most of the event near steady state). At ACH = 0.5, the correction is ~0.54 (concentration is still building throughout most of the event).

Note that ACH and room volume jointly determine the total airflow Q = ACH × V (m³/h). At steady state, infection risk depends on Q — not on ACH or V independently. See the room volume evidence page.

Is the default of 3.0 ACH reasonable?

The default of 3.0 represents moderate-to-good mechanical ventilation. For context:

Residences (US)0.35ASHRAE 62.2
Residences (Germany)0.6Meta-analysis below

Aganovic et al. (2023) used 0.5 ACH as “normal ventilation” (typical home environment) and 3 ACH as “enhanced ventilation” (hospital areas and public buildings). Similarly, Alsved et al. (2023) compared 0.5 and 3 ACH in their indoor air model.

Most documented COVID-19 outbreaks occurred in spaces with ACH well below 1 (Peng et al. 2022, Table 1: Guangzhou restaurant 0.67, Skagit choir 0.70, call center 0.53). A default of 3.0 therefore represents a better-than-typical event venue, which is appropriate for a conservative risk estimate.

Evidence for the ACH–risk relationship

Infection probability curves at different ACH values

Aganovic et al. (2023) Figure 5a shows infection probability over 0–360 min for Omicron speaking at different ACH levels (64 m² classroom, 1 infected):

ACHRisk at 180 minRisk at 360 min
0.5~23%~40%
2.0~12%~20%
6.0~7%~12%
12.0~4%~7%

The reduction from 0.5 to 2 ACH (4×) cuts risk roughly in half, but going from 6 to 12 ACH (2×) only cuts it by about 40%. This reflects the diminishing returns inherent in the exponential dose-response: at low doses, risk is approximately proportional to 1/ACH, but at higher doses the relationship saturates.

Meta-analysis: ACH and airborne SARS-CoV-2 concentration

A meta-analysis of 93 studies covering hospitals and residences across 24 countries found a negative correlation between ACH and airborne virus concentration. Typical hospital ACH (6–16) was associated with significantly lower virus positivity rates than residential ACH (0.35–0.6). Benefits plateaued above ~8 ACH — further increases produced minimal additional concentration reduction.

CFD study: diminishing returns at higher ACH

A 2024 CFD study modeled a 140 m³ classroom at 2, 4, 6, and 8 ACH. Increasing ACH from 2 to 8 reduced particle inhalation by ~70%, but the gains flattened at higher values (steeper improvement from 2→4 than from 6→8). The study also found that recirculation percentage matters: reducing recirculation from 50% to 30% significantly reduced particle concentration, because recirculated unfiltered air returns virus to the room.

Limitations

ACH is treated as effective removal rate

The model uses ACH as the sole removal mechanism. In reality, total removal includes ventilation, gravitational deposition, viral inactivation, and respiratory absorption (Aganovic et al. 2023 models nine separate mechanisms). At ACH ≥ 3, ventilation dominates the other terms and the simplification is reasonable. At lower ACH, deposition and inactivation become proportionally more significant and the model may overestimate risk.

Fresh air vs. recirculated air

The model does not distinguish between fresh outside air and recirculated air. If an HVAC system recirculates air without effective filtration, the virus is returned to the room and the effective removal rate is lower than the nominal ACH. Users should enter the effective ACH for virus removal: for 100% outside air or HEPA-filtered recirculation, use the full ACH; for unfiltered recirculation, use only the outside air fraction.

Mixing ventilation assumed

As noted on the room volume evidence page, the model assumes mixing ventilation. For displacement or natural ventilation, the same ACH value produces different spatial concentration patterns and the model’s predictions may need adjustment.
