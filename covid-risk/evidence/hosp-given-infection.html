COVID event risk calculator

Evidence and methodology for model inputs

P(hospitalization | infection)

The model uses separate hospitalization-per-infection tables for IC attendees (stratified by severity and vaccination status) and non-IC attendees (stratified by vaccination status only).

How it affects the model

These values convert expected infections into expected hospitalizations. They are applied after the Wells-Riley infection probability is computed, so they do not affect the infection estimate itself — only the downstream severity outcomes.

Deriving the baseline: non-IC IHR

CDC burden estimates (2024–2025 season)

The CDC estimates ~14.1–20.7M symptomatic illnesses and ~390–550K hospitalizations for the 2024–2025 season, giving a symptomatic IHR of ~2.7% across all ages. After adjusting for 30–50% asymptomatic infections (see community infectious evidence), the all-infection IHR is ~1.3–1.9%.

However, hospitalizations are heavily concentrated in older adults. CDC COVID-NET data for 2024–2025 shows hospitalization rates per 100K of ~10 for ages 18–49 vs ~300+ for ages 75+. For this event’s young-skewing demographics (90% under 60), the age-adjusted non-IC IHR is substantially lower than the population average.

The 18–49 all-vaccination-status IHR is ~0.32%. Decomposing by vaccination status using VE against hospitalization (45% current-season, ~15% older vaccination) and adjusting for “for COVID” vs “with COVID” admissions (~65% primary diagnosis) gives:

Infection rates are age-independent

The seroprevalence study also reported infection-to-case ratios of 2.2–3.7× across all adult age groups during Omicron, confirming that susceptibility to infection does not vary dramatically by age. The age effect is almost entirely in severity, not in infection probability. This justifies applying age multipliers to outcome rates rather than to the Wells-Riley infection probability.

IC model values

The IC hospitalization table is derived by applying INFORM severity-tier multipliers to the non-IC baseline, then adjusting for IC-specific VE (~36–40% for current-season vaccine vs 45% for non-IC). Values are set between two anchors:

  • Lower anchor (INFORM, population-based): non-IC baseline × aIRR. This uses all infections (including undetected) as the denominator.
  • Upper anchor (cancer cohort studies): 9–24% hospitalization among detected Omicron cases in cancer patients, adjusted ÷3 for undetected infections → ~3–8%.

The model values split the difference, leaning toward the INFORM population-based estimates since they better match the model’s per-infection denominator:

Cross-check: model-implied IC IHR vs INFORM

With the default IC vax mix (8% PrEP, 30% current vax, 62% old vax) and severity mix (5/20/75%), the model produces a blended IC IHR of ~1.0%. The INFORM study found IC individuals accounted for 3.9% of the population but 22% of hospitalizations, implying an IC IHR roughly 5–6× the population average. At a population IHR of ~1.5%, this gives IC IHR ≈ 7.5–9% for the all-ages INFORM cohort. The model’s 1.0% is much lower, which is expected because: (a) this event’s IC population skews younger and more vaccinated than the INFORM all-ages cohort (median age ~60), and (b) the corrected severity mix (5/20/75%) is dominated by mildly IC individuals (see IC prevalence evidence), who have near-baseline hospitalization rates.

Limitations

  • No direct measurement of P(hosp | infection) for IC subgroups. The literature provides relative risks (IC vs general population), not absolute infection-to-hospitalization ratios by IC severity and vaccination status. The model’s values are derived by combining the non-IC baseline with IC multipliers, which compounds uncertainties from both.
  • INFORM aIRRs are from England 2022–2023. The UK NHS proactively vaccinates IC patients (70% with ≥4 doses), so these ratios reflect a well-vaccinated IC cohort. In the US, where IC vaccination rates are lower (~30% current season), the unvaccinated IC risk may be underrepresented in the multipliers.
  • Three severity tiers are a coarse simplification. The INFORM data shows a 10× difference between the highest-risk (stem cell transplant, aIRR 14.2) and lowest-risk (solid tumors, aIRR 1.4) IC conditions. Grouping them into three buckets loses resolution.
  • Vaccination effect on IC patients is uncertain. VE against hospitalization in IC adults (36–40%) is measured for IC as a group, not stratified by IC severity. Severe IC conditions (transplant) may have much lower VE than mild IC conditions (solid tumors).
  • PrEP (Pemgarda) effectiveness data is very limited. Only 48 patients in the largest real-world study. The additional reduction factor for vax+PrEP is estimated, not measured with precision.
