COVID event risk calculator

Evidence and methodology for model inputs

IC self-selection factor

Default: 0.70 (immunocompromised people attend at 70% the rate of the general population).

How it affects the model

The model estimates expected IC attendees by multiplying the number of total attendees by age-specific IC prevalence rates. The self-selection factor then scales this down to account for the fact that IC individuals are less likely to attend indoor events than the general population — due to health limitations, medical advice, or deliberate risk avoidance.

A factor of 1.0 means IC people attend at the same rate as everyone else. A factor of 0.5 means they attend at half the rate. This directly scales all IC-specific outputs (expected IC attendees, IC infections, IC hospitalizations).

Evidence

No direct measurement exists

No peer-reviewed study directly measures the rate at which immunocompromised people attend indoor social events compared to the general population. The default of 0.70 is derived from indirect proxies.

Quality-of-life and social functioning proxies

Several studies measure social participation limitations in IC populations using validated instruments:

  • Cavallini et al. (2015) — integrative review of quality of life in solid organ transplant (SOT) recipients. SF-36 social functioning subscales show reduced social participation, particularly in the first year post-transplant and during active immunosuppressive adjustment.
  • Baumgartner et al. (2017) — quality of life in common variable immunodeficiency (CVID) patients. Documented reduced social functioning scores compared to population norms.
  • Heesen et al. (2022) — CoCo longitudinal study of COVID-era behavior in chronically ill populations. Found sustained avoidance of social gatherings among immunocompromised individuals beyond what the general population reported.

Population-weighted estimate

The model’s IC severity mix is 22% severe, 55% moderate, 23% mild. Weighting the attendance ratios by severity gives a pre-pandemic baseline of ~0.80–0.90.

COVID-era avoidance behavior adds an additional reduction. Islam et al. (2021) documented sustained avoidance of social gatherings among high-risk individuals. Applying a ~0.85× COVID-era multiplier gives a current-era range of ~0.50–0.85, with 0.70 as the midpoint.

Limitations

  • All estimates are indirect. No study has directly measured IC attendance at events. The proxies (SF-36 social functioning, IMET scores, behavioral surveys) were designed to measure general social participation, not event attendance specifically.
  • Highly event-dependent. An IC person’s willingness to attend depends on the specific event: a small outdoor gathering is very different from a large indoor conference. The model applies a single factor regardless of event characteristics.
  • COVID-era behavior is still evolving. The additional avoidance multiplier from the pandemic era may be waning as COVID becomes endemic, or it may persist among the most vulnerable. There is no current data on the trajectory.
  • Selection bias in both directions. IC people who do attend events may be the healthiest subset (survivor bias), meaning the ones present are at lower IC-related risk than the IC population average. Conversely, the most severely IC individuals who avoid events entirely are not captured by the model at all.

Given the substantial uncertainty, this parameter is left as a tunable slider. The defensible range is 0.50–0.85; the default of 0.70 represents a middle estimate. Users who know their event’s specific IC community (e.g., a support group for transplant recipients vs. a general tech conference) should adjust accordingly.
